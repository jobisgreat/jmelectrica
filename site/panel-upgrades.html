A lot of Boston homes, from Dorchester triple-deckers to South Boston row houses, are still running on panels sized for a few lights and a fridge. If you're adding an EV charger, a heat pump, central air or a new kitchen, the panel is usually the first thing that needs to change. When homeowners call us for this

  • You still have a fuse box, or breakers trip when two appliances run at once
  • Your panel is a brand electricians flag as a known hazard (Federal Pacific Stab-Lok or Zinsco)
  • You're planning an EV charger, heat pump, central A/C, or kitchen or bath renovation
  • There's no room left in the panel, or you see double-tapped breakers and tandem breakers everywhere
  • Lights dim when the A/C or microwave kicks on, or the panel feels warm or buzzes
  • An insurer or home inspector has flagged your panel

What's involved

  1. Load assessment

    We look at what's on your panel today and what you plan to add, then size the new panel and service (commonly 100 to 200 amps) to fit. No upselling: if your current panel can handle it, we'll say so.

  2. Permit and utility coordination

    Panel and service upgrades in Boston need an electrical permit and a city inspection. When the service size changes, the utility also has to disconnect and reconnect power. We handle the paperwork and scheduling so you're not chasing anyone.

  3. The swap

    Power goes off, the old panel comes out, and a new panel with new breakers goes in. Every circuit is labeled so you know what each breaker controls.

  4. Inspection and walkthrough

    After the inspector signs off, we walk you through the new panel, show you the labeling, and leave the area cleaner than we found it.

Panel Upgrades FAQ

How do I know if I need a panel upgrade or just a few new circuits?

Not every home needs a full upgrade. We do a load calculation based on what you have and what you're adding. Sometimes a few new breakers or a subpanel does the job, and we'll tell you if that's the case.

Will my power be off all day?

Power is off for part of the installation day while the old panel is removed and the new one is connected. We'll give you a realistic window ahead of time so you can plan for the fridge, work-from-home, and anyone on medical equipment.

Do you pull the permit?

Yes. Panel and service work requires a permit and inspection in Massachusetts. We file it under our license (#22667) and schedule the inspection.

I'm thinking about an EV charger or heat pump. Should I upgrade first?

Often, yes, but not always. Tell us everything you're planning and we'll size the panel once, so you're not paying for two upgrades.
