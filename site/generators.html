When homeowners call us for this

  • You lose heat, a sump pump or a fridge full of food when the power goes out
  • Someone at home relies on medical equipment
  • You use a portable generator with extension cords (which is risky) and want a safe hookup
  • You work from home and can't afford downtime

What's involved

  1. Decide what to power

    We go through what you need on during an outage and size the system to match, so you're not overbuying.

  2. Choose the setup

    A portable generator with an inlet and interlock or transfer switch is the budget option. A permanently installed standby generator starts automatically. We'll explain both.

  3. Install

    We install the transfer switch or interlock, inlet and wiring. For standby units on natural gas or propane, the gas connection must be made by a licensed gas fitter; we'll tell you exactly what's needed.

  4. Permit and test

    We file the electrical permit, test a full switchover, and show you how it works.

Generators FAQ

Standby or portable: which do I need?

If you want the house to switch over by itself and power a lot of circuits, standby. If you're comfortable rolling out a generator and powering essentials, a portable unit with a proper interlock or transfer switch is much cheaper.

Can I just plug my generator into an outlet?

No. Backfeeding through an outlet is dangerous to you and to utility workers. An inlet with an interlock or transfer switch is the safe, code-compliant way.

Do you handle the gas line?

Gas connections must be done by a licensed gas fitter. We handle all the electrical work and will be clear about what the gas work involves.
